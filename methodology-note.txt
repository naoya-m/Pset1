1. HOW THE SITE WAS BUILT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gemini Deep Research was used first, to list the data that could realistically
be collected on tourism, municipal finance, and the local economy and community
in Yugawara Town. The result was a datasheet of 19 candidate indicators across
six themes - macro tourism dynamics, tourism spending and the economy, sites
and events, transport and local movement, leading demand indicators, and local
hosting capacity - each recorded with:

      - the name of the indicator
      - the data source and the body that publishes it
      - update frequency, and spatial and temporal granularity
      - the format it is published in, and how it can be obtained
      - how it would be used on the dashboard, and why it matters for policy
      - citations and URLs

Claude Code was then used to collect the data, by the methods set out in
section 2. Of the 19 candidate indicators, 17 series were obtained; the reasons
the rest could not be are given in the "Data and limitations" section of the
site.

From those series, several dashboard concepts were drafted, each aimed at a
decision a tourism organisation or a local government would actually have to
take. Four were taken forward and assessed with Claude on two axes:

      meaningful	does the insight say something true and non-obvious
                       	about Yugawara, rather than restating a known figure?
      actionable   	can someone act on it - is there a decision, a budget
                       	or a policy lever attached to the insight?

The concept scoring highest on both was an accommodation tax simulator: the
town is actively considering such a tax, the data speaks to it directly, and
the output is a rate rather than an observation. That became the site's first
tab. Two of the other concepts were kept as supporting tabs - the evidence for
why a tax is needed, and the evidence for what the revenue should be spent on -
and the fourth tab documents the data and its limits. The site was then built
in Claude Code.


2. HOW THE DATA WAS COLLECTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All data comes from primary sources. No secondary citations, blogs or
aggregator sites were used.

  PDF text extraction
      Kanagawa Prefecture Tourist Visitor Survey; Yugawara Town Statistical
      Handbook; the town's accommodation tax study report. The text layer was
      read with PDFKit (Swift, macOS) and reshaped into tables in Python. Two
      tables whose layout does not survive extraction - Tables 4 and 5 of the
      prefectural survey - were transcribed against rendered page images.

  Official published files
      e-Stat (2020 Population Census; 2021 Economic Census for Business
      Activity) and JR East station boarding statistics, taken directly as
      published CSV/Excel.

  Screen-recording transcription
      RESAS credit-card spending and overnight-guest analyses offer no CSV
      export and no public API for these views. The screen was recorded while
      the pointer traced every month; frames were cut at 0.5-second intervals,
      tooltips were detected and cropped automatically, and the values read
      off. About one minute of recording yields twelve months of one series.

External dependencies are limited to macOS Swift/PDFKit and the Python standard
library. The series taken from PDFs and official files are regenerated by
data/build_csv.py and data/build_csv2.py; the two RESAS series come from the
transcription step above and are not scripted. build_submit.py converts the
whole set into the English submission dataset.


3. VERIFICATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Identity checks, run on every month and every year:
        annual total   = sum of the 12 months
        total visitors = overnight + day visitors
        total spend    = sum of its components
    Differences of up to +/-10 are accepted where the original publication
    rounds its own components.

  * Cross-checks. Annual figures rebuilt from the monthly table agree with two
    other published tables and with the figures in the town's own study report.

  * Series joining. 2005-2009 (town handbook) and 2010-2024 (prefectural
    survey) overlap in 2015 and agree exactly - 3,109k total / 568k overnight /
    2,541k day - so they are treated as a single series.

  * A rejected substitute. The RESAS overnight-guest analysis was tested as a
    stand-in for municipal guest-night totals and rejected: its year-on-year
    direction disagrees with the prefectural survey in 7 of 11 years, and the
    ratio between the two wanders between 1.01 and 1.59 with no stable
    conversion factor. It is used only as a share within its own source.
